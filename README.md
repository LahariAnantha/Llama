Constraints: This project relies on three publicly available government datasets that measure different aspects of the economy. One limitation is that the Consumer Price Index (CPI) represents price changes experienced only by urban consumers. Therefore, it may not fully represent the price experiences and spending patterns of rural consumers and all demographic groups. Additionally the CPI is calculated using sampled pieces rather than prices from every transaction, making the estimates subject to sampling error. Because the index includes food and energy prices, even small-term fluctuations can complicate the interpretation of underlying inflation trends. Therefore the analysis can only establish associations between these economic indicators but cannot prove causation. Inflation and employment are not the only factors affecting consumer spending. Changes in things like wages, interest rates and consumer confidence and seasonal shopping patterns can also cause changes in retail sales. Another limitation is that the Census Monthly Retail Trade Survey measures sales in dollar amounts rather than the quantity of goods purchased. Increases in small retail sales may reflect higher prices rather than an actual increase in purchasing activity. The datasets also have different definitions, unit and reporting procedures, which require a careful alignment of dates and consistent data preprocessing. Seasonal patterns may affect prices, sales and unemployment measurements differently, so we will need to verify the seasonal adjustment status of each series before comparing them.
